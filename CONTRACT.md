# Terrace product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Terrace is a US residential property-management system joining leasing, online rent collection, trust accounting, owner and resident portals and completed maintenance in one reconciled ledger. It is the full product a buyer of AppFolio, Buildium, Rent Manager or Yardi Breeze can switch to, with the accessible lifecycle of DoorLoop and TurboTenant and continuing portfolio agents beyond them.

This contract describes the product being built towards. Missing capability never reduces the destination. Each clause states buyer behavior and a public-door acceptance journey; [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) lists every journey with its current status. Only the final Status table claims implementation. Status is judged from current code and tests, beginning with the 16 October audit at `a036d57fe351a1e2b74f46ca4333cbe531406459`; domain files and product tests are unchanged since that audit, while current Base/auth/runtime changes were read separately. The published core retains the five existing release journeys and their `ACCEPTANCE-1` through `ACCEPTANCE-5` capability references; this contract introduces clause IDs without renaming those references.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Residential rental property management software |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | AppFolio, Buildium, Rent Manager, Yardi Breeze, DoorLoop, TurboTenant |
| First buyer | A US independent third-party residential property manager with approximately 50–500 units, collecting USD rent and deposits for multiple owners, with leasing and maintenance staff and a bookkeeper/accountant |

**The complete job.** Keep homes occupied, collect and account for rent and deposits, complete repairs, pay vendors and owners, and keep residents, owners and staff informed through acquisition, lease, renewal, move-out and close. Terrace owns the residential subledgers and financial control experience, even when specialist providers execute the regulated rails. It includes all inventory table stakes, named integrations, follow-up and push-further work. US federal/state/local rule packs are reviewed and versioned with professionals; jurisdictional engineering and provider agreements are requirements rather than exclusions.

**Products stay separate.** [Cabin](https://github.com/just-every/run-cabin) owns vacation-rental reservations and channel management; [Pantry](https://github.com/just-every/run-pantry) owns general business accounting; [Inkwell](https://github.com/just-every/run-inkwell) owns general electronic signatures; [Glove](https://github.com/just-every/run-glove) is the trade business’s field-service system; [Gasket](https://github.com/just-every/run-gasket) owns a maintenance team’s CMMS; [Chest](https://github.com/just-every/run-chest) owns general cloud file sync; [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit) owns sales CRM; [Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) owns cross-tool automation. Terrace integrates them while retaining its full residential workflows, portals and reconciled ledger. Unowned category gaps are not new Terrace products.

## Operating rules

- Fresh installations start empty. Operators enter real records or perform a reviewed migration; production never fabricates data, provider results, delivery or completion.
- Target money is USD in exact cents, with explicit effective dates, allocation and rounding. New tenancies use USD; records entered before PORT-001 remain “currency unconfirmed” until the installation owner confirms AUD or USD once, without converting amounts (PORT-001).
- Approval identifies the exact version, amount, recipient/payee, destination and authority. Changed inputs invalidate approval. Scheduled debits require a person-authorized mandate; owners/managers approve spend and payouts, and agents never approve themselves.
- Financial history is append-only with reversals/corrections. Duplicate requests, restarts, provider callbacks and migration replays cannot create a second charge, payment, payout or filing.
- A draft is not delivery, a submitted payment is not settled cash, an appointment is not a repair, and a closed vendor status is not resident-confirmed resolution. Uncertain results stay open with an accountable next action.
- Authenticated roles and property boundaries apply at every server door. Sensitive screening, tax and bank data are restricted independently of ordinary operational access.
- Existing security, audit, custody, portability and erasure guarantees remain binding. No existing BASE clause is superseded by this target. The published first-release exclusions of collection, trust accounting, listings, screening and delivery describe present gaps only; the target clauses below require their complete implementation.

## Published clauses — retained first-release behavior

### Published operating core

- **TER-001 — Sign in and control operator access.** An owner invites managers (admin) and preparers (member). Managers record and approve property work; preparers read and propose but cannot approve, post rent, import, erase or restore. Owner-only data and access operations check the live role. Same-origin mutation checks and session revocation apply through every existing command and download. **Acceptance evidence:** Use ordinary invitation and sign-in, attempt protected commands as a preparer, submit a cross-origin command, revoke a manager and retry the old session; forbidden requests make no changes.

- **TER-002 — Keep linked tenancy terms.** Managers create a tenancy with address, owner and tenant contacts, lease dates, rent and cadence. The tenancy list opens that tenancy’s detail with its actions carrying the selected identity. Each tenancy stores its currency: new tenancies are USD and records entered before PORT-001 are “currency unconfirmed” until the installation owner confirms AUD or USD, never converted (PORT-001). **Acceptance evidence:** Create a tenancy through the command API, open its linked row in /app, and verify detail forms address that tenancy and its saved terms.

- **TER-003 — Approve exact renewal revisions.** Preparers propose a later lease end and rent with a source reference. An owner or manager approves or rejects the pending proposal; approval retains previous terms and the actual decision actor. Competing proposals based on the old tenancy version cannot overwrite the accepted terms. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare two renewals as a member, refuse member approval, approve one as manager, and show that the second is stale while prior terms and approver remain retained.

- **TER-004 — Record external rent accurately.** Managers record dated charges, already received external receipts and explained charge reductions in integer cents. Unique references prevent duplication; concurrent receipts cannot exceed the unpaid charge. Recording a receipt does not claim that Terrace collected or settled money. **Acceptance evidence:** Post a charge and partial receipt, replay the reference, race two further receipts, reduce the charge with a reason and read the resulting balance through /api/state.

- **TER-005 — Prepare dated arrears decisions.** Preparers draft an arrears message and follow-up date against a tenancy. Only an owner or manager decides the proposal and the decision retains the actor. An approved draft remains distinct from a delivered message or a resolved delinquency. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare an arrears follow-up as a member, approve it as the owner and inspect its saved message, due date and approval through the tenancy state.

- **TER-006 — Export period rent receivables.** Managers download a tenancy-period owner CSV with opening receivable, charges, external receipts, explained reductions and closing receivable. It states its recorded-rent boundary; it is not yet a trust, bank, distribution or tax statement. Preparers cannot download it. **Acceptance evidence:** Download a statement spanning the charge and receipts, reconcile its opening plus movements to closing, find receipt references, and refuse the same download to a member.

- **TER-007 — Retain photographed inspections.** Managers create dated inspections with notes, upload real JPEG/PNG bytes and complete only with notes and photo evidence. Authenticated photo retrieval preserves the stored bytes and fingerprint; completed inspections retain their tenancy relationship. **Acceptance evidence:** Refuse an empty completion and invalid image, upload a photo through multipart API, retrieve identical bytes and complete the inspection with notes.

- **TER-008 — Approve an external trade handoff.** A preparer records maintenance, trade contact, due date and estimate. Managers approve the exact revision before downloading an unsent .eml packet, then record a reference to the human’s external handoff. A packet download never counts as provider delivery or repair completion. **Acceptance evidence:** Refuse packet retrieval before approval, approve the current job once, inspect its addressed packet and record an external Sent-folder reference with the approval fingerprint retained.

- **TER-009 — Review and undo tenancy CSV move-in.** The owner previews Terrace tenancy CSV v1 under a stable namespace, approves its exact fingerprint, retains original bytes and a journal, and undoes unchanged imports. Undo refuses to erase tenancies with later rent, work or edits. This kit is not an incumbent importer. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview the published CSV, reject an incorrect fingerprint, apply the reviewed preview, inspect originals and journal, undo an untouched batch and refuse undo after a later charge.

- **TER-010 — Export and restore product custody.** The owner exports all current product rows, photos, original imports and journals to a .rer archive and restores into an empty product destination. The destination retains its own owner identity; sessions and private configuration are not restored. Nonempty destinations and corrupt archives are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Download /api/backup and restore through data.restore into a fresh installation; compare tenancy, ledger and photo bytes, exercise retained import undo and refuse a second restore into occupied data.

- **TER-011 — Erase tenancy custody with a restore fence.** The owner confirms the exact tenant email before erasing the tenancy, its dependent product data, photos and import custody. A later archive carries an erasure journal that blocks resurrection from an older archive when supplied during restore. Offline archives require that later journal to know about later erasure. **Acceptance evidence:** Reject an incorrect confirmation, erase the tenancy, verify absent state and photo 404, export the later journal and refuse an older archive plus that journal in a clean destination.

- **TER-012 — Preserve the installed app and run site.** The installed root remains the shared Lobby. /app keeps linked tenancies and existing rent, work, move-in and data/access forms with a stacked phone ledger. The separate public run site builds the approved homepage, Run Terrace, Markdown guides and real not-found document without reading installation data. **Acceptance evidence:** Build the site and open /, /run, /docs and a missing path; in the signed-in app use linked tenancy actions and inspect the rent ledger at 1440 and 390 pixels with real entered records.

## Target clauses — complete residential management

### Portfolio, leases and operating rights

- **PORT-001 — Run a US portfolio in USD.** Set up a US management company, legal entities, office contacts, EIN reference, property timezones and USD operating accounts. Dates and schedules use property-local time and money uses exact cents with explicit rounding. Preserve legacy AUD history as AUD with a reviewed migration rather than relabeling currency. Exact semantics: every money record (tenancy rent, charge, receipt, charge reduction, renewal history and maintenance estimate) stores its ISO 4217 currency. A new tenancy is USD; an imported historical tenancy may be AUD and stays AUD. Records entered before this rule were labelled A$ and then $ without a stored currency, so their currency is unconfirmed: a tenancy that existed when Terrace began storing currencies (migration `0018_us_portfolio.sql` marked it AUD) is “currency unconfirmed” unless it was imported with a property time zone (only a PORT-001 tenancy CSV sets one, with an explicit currency), and a transfer of an unconfirmed lease is unconfirmed too. Its money, and every charge, receipt, reduction, renewal, estimate, term, schedule and asset of it, is shown as the amount followed by “(currency unconfirmed)”, never as A$ or $, and every total (the Tenancies home, the rent roll and the collection view) lists unconfirmed money as its own line, never summed with USD or AUD. The installation owner, and only the owner, confirms once per installation on Data & access that those records were AUD or USD; the confirmation is recorded with who, when and how many tenancies, audited, and refused a second time. Confirming AUD marks them confirmed AUD; confirming USD relabels them and their records USD. Either way no amount changes and no conversion is claimed. Apart from that one confirmation, a record’s currency never changes, a charge, receipt or reduction carries its tenancy’s, amounts are never converted and totals are kept per currency. Amounts are integer cents: an entry with more than two decimal places is refused, never rounded. Each tenancy stores its property’s IANA time zone: a US zone when created or changed (defaulting to the office zone), any IANA zone for an imported historical tenancy, and UTC for records entered before this rule until a manager sets one. Due, received, lease and follow-up dates are property-local calendar days; “today”, overdue balances and the future-receipt check use the property-local day; a recorded instant counts on the property-local day it falls on; and a statement period runs from the start of its first day to the start of the day after its last day, each shown with that day’s UTC offset. The company’s office time zone (America/New_York until the company sets one) dates the Tenancies home. An EIN is a nine-digit reference checked for format only, never verified with the IRS; an operating account is a recorded USD reference (legal entity, name, bank and last four digits) with no full account or routing number, and Terrace moves no money from it. A property (PORT-002) carries its zone and a tenancy placed on one of its units takes it; a tenancy without a unit record carries its own. PAY-001 and every later clause that adds a money record, schedule or date-bearing record inherit the stored-currency and property-local rules before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a US company and two property timezones, enter USD rent, cross a daylight-saving boundary and import an AUD historical tenancy; show currency and period boundaries without converting or relabeling old balances.

- **PORT-002 — Separate properties buildings and units.** Maintain portfolios, buildings, addresses, unit identifiers, bedrooms, amenities, occupancy state and rentable status independently from tenancies. Units retain past and future leases; transfers never merge two units or lose prior history. Exact semantics: a portfolio holds properties; a property (a building of kind single-family, duplex, multifamily, townhouse or condo) has an address and a US time zone; a unit has an identifier unique within its property, bedrooms, bathrooms in halves, amenities and a rentable flag. A tenancy placed on a unit takes the unit’s address and its property’s time zone; a tenancy recorded without a unit keeps its own address. A unit holds at most one lease on any day (co-tenants share one lease, PORT-004), and a unit that is not rentable takes no new lease. A lease occupies its unit from its start to its move-out day when ended early, otherwise to its end date; ending early keeps the record and the original end date. A transfer ends the lease the day before the move-in and starts a linked lease on the other unit with the same parties, rent and end date unless changed. Occupancy is read as of the property-local today: occupied, vacant with a future lease, or vacant. MOVE-001 importers, REPORT-001, ROLE-001 property scoping and PORT-006 move-in and move-out inherit units before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a duplex with two units, end one lease, move a resident to the other unit and view each unit’s dated occupancy and lease history.

- **PORT-003 — Manage owners and management agreements.** Track individual/entity owners, joint ownership shares with effective dates, W-9 collection status, management agreements, reserve requirements and property allocations. A mid-period ownership change preserves who earned income and owed expenses before and after transfer. Exact semantics: an owner is an individual or entity with a legal name, email and mailing address, kept apart from a tenancy’s recorded owner contact, which is never rewritten. W-9 status is not requested, requested on a date, or received on a date with where the signed form is kept, recorded by staff; Terrace stores no taxpayer identification number and never claims TIN or IRS validation. Each property has dated share sets in basis points that total exactly 100%; the set in force on a day is the one with the latest effective date on or before it, so a transfer recorded from a date ends the seller’s share the day before and starts the buyer’s that day, and a new set must start after the latest one (history is never edited). A management agreement per property records its start, the fee as a percent of collected rent, the reserve requirement in USD and its document reference; the agreement in force on a day is the latest started on or before it. The reserve requirement is an amount owed to reserves, not cash held (ACCT-001 and ACCT-010 carry held reserves). Each recorded receipt on a tenancy on the property (except receipts on ACCT-016 tax charges, which are never owner income) is allocated by the share set in force on its received date: each owner gets the floor of the exact cents times their share, and any remainder cents go to the largest share (ties to the owner listed first); the fee is the agreement percent of each allocated amount, rounded half up to the cent. Each owner’s reserve share is the requirement split the same way, per stretch of the period with one share set and one agreement in force. An owner allocation statement for an owner and an inclusive period lists each allocated receipt with its share, fee and net, totals per currency, and the reserve shares, on each side of any transfer date. Owner records, W-9 status, share sets and agreements are installation-wide records kept by the owner and all-portfolio portfolio managers (ROLE-001); bookkeepers, accountants and managers download allocation statements for properties in their portfolios, and leasing staff, maintenance coordinators and members see owner names and shares only. Expenses are allocated the same way when vendor bills ship (ACCT-007) and full owner statements carry them (ACCT-012). **Acceptance evidence:** Add two owners with shares, change ownership on a dated transfer and inspect the income, reserve and statement allocation on each side of that date.

- **PORT-004 — Keep resident households and occupants.** Separate residents, co-tenants, guarantors, nonpaying occupants, emergency contacts and authorized contacts; track communication preferences and household membership dates. A person on multiple leases has distinct liabilities and portal access for each. Exact semantics: a lease’s tenant is its primary resident; its household adds co-tenants, guarantors, occupants, emergency contacts and authorized contacts, each with contact details, a contact preference (email, phone, mail or none) and the days they joined and left. The primary resident, co-tenants and guarantors are liable for that lease’s balance; occupants and contacts are not. The recipients of lease notices and statements are the current primary resident, co-tenants, guarantors and authorized contacts whose preference is not none; the current primary resident, co-tenants, guarantors and authorized contacts hold lease document rights whatever their preference; emergency contacts and occupants have neither. Current means joined on or before the property-local today and not yet left. A person on two leases has a separate household row on each, so liabilities and rights are per lease. Moving a member ends their membership the day before the move and adds them to the other lease from that day; each lease keeps its balance. Erasing a tenancy erases its household. Terrace lists recipients and sends nothing: PORTAL-005 and PORTAL-009 deliver to this list, and PORTAL-001 portal access and PORT-007 documents enforce these rights, before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Add co-tenants and a guarantor, transfer one person between leases and verify balances, recipients and document rights stay with the correct household.

- **PORT-005 — Version complete lease obligations.** Record signed lease versions, recurring rent schedules, proration, concessions, deposits, pets, utilities, parking, insurance, addenda and any Housing Choice Voucher HAP contract (housing authority, tenant portion and HAP portion, PAY-013) with effective dates and source documents. Amendments preserve old terms and preview their future charges before approval. Exact semantics: an owner or portfolio manager records an original signed PDF (at most 5 MB), its signer names, signed date and source reference, together with the complete dated rent, utility, pet, parking and concession schedule and deposit, pet, utility, parking, insurance, addendum and HAP obligations (explicitly “none” where absent). HAP terms name the housing authority, contract reference and exact tenant/HAP portions; PAY-013 owns posting and reconciliation of those payer splits. This is staff-recorded documentary evidence, never an assertion that Terrace collected or verified signatures (LEASE-011 supplies electronic execution). Each charge term and obligation names its source clause; amounts remain in the confirmed tenancy currency, never converted, and an unconfirmed historical currency must be confirmed under PORT-001 first. Versions take effect within the lease, after the last approved version; their signed original, payload and approved history never change. Preparation records no charge. The preview runs PAY-001’s property-local, inclusive-day proration and half-up cent rounding over the effective schedule; a concession reduces only rent covering its own interval. Approval binds the exact source, version, current tenancy, complete term snapshot and preview fingerprint and requires another approver under ROLE-003, with its sole-operator label. It atomically ends earlier terms the day before the effective date, supersedes unposted future terms and activates the prepared schedule; a changed source or terms needs a new version, and a competing version or posted period at/after the effective date refuses stale approval. The ordinary charge run then posts the previewed lines once, retaining the lease-version id and source clause in the resident ledger. Legacy add/amend-term commands cannot bypass a signed lease’s review, and a prepared signed version is later operating work that prevents silent import undo. Private sources and complete version data are available only to the owner and in-scope portfolio managers, bookkeepers and accountants; only the owner and portfolio managers prepare, preview or approve. Leasing staff read version dates, signers, references and obligation kind/date/source metadata without charge or HAP amounts or original downloads. Other roles cannot download signed sources; every source download is audited and no-store. SAFE-004 lease retention and legal holds preserve sources until eligible erasure, which removes originals and version text, remembers erased hashes and fences older archives; SAFE-005’s readable export contains original PDFs with hashes and restores full versions and terms. PORT-007 inherits broader searchable/versioned document custody, LEASE-012 approved renewal activation writes a versioned schedule, PAY-013 inherits HAP version/source links, and ACCT-003 inherits the versioned deposit obligation before those capabilities ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Enter a lease with a concession and pet charge, approve a mid-month amendment, and reconcile previewed charges and source clauses to the resident ledger while retaining the signed prior version.

- **PORT-006 — Complete move-in transfers and move-out.** Use checklists for keys, meters, condition, prorated charges, deposits, forwarding address and possession. Roommate changes and unit transfers close and reopen the appropriate obligations; move-out produces the deposit disposition and make-ready work without deleting the lease. **Acceptance evidence:** Move a household in mid-month, transfer units and move out; verify prorations, distinct deposits, final balances, keys checklist and linked turnover tasks.

- **PORT-007 — Store searchable operating documents.** Attach and version leases, applications, notices, inspection photos/video, work documents and invoices on their records. Search authorized content and metadata; restrict screening and tax documents, scan uploads and preserve download history and retention classes. ROLE-001 applies before this ships: document search and downloads return only records on tenancies in the searcher’s portfolios and role. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload an amended lease, video and restricted screening PDF; find the current version from its unit, recover the prior lease and deny a vendor and unrelated resident access. A scoped staff user’s search and direct document download return nothing from another portfolio (404). SAFE-005 applies before this ships: each stored document, photo or video appears in the readable export as its original file with its SHA-256 and restores with the backup. PORT-005 signed originals, versions, source clauses and retention remain intact when broader document search and custody ship.

- **PORT-008 — Apply named state landlord-tenant rule packs.** Terrace ships a versioned, counsel-reviewed rule pack for each of the 50 states and the District of Columbia, plus local overlays where a city or county adds deposit, late-fee, notice or rent rules (first: Los Angeles, San Francisco, New York City, Chicago and Seattle). Packs activate in this order: California, Texas and Florida; then New York, Georgia, North Carolina, Arizona, Illinois, Washington and Colorado; then every remaining state and DC. Each pack states, with statute citation, effective date, version and reviewer: security-deposit cap; separate-account, bank-disclosure and receipt requirements; deposit interest rate and payment schedule; refund and itemization deadlines; required disposition delivery method and address rule (personal delivery, first-class mail, certified mail); late-fee cap, fee basis and grace period; partial-payment effects on notices; pay-or-quit and lease-violation notice periods and permitted service methods; rent-increase notice periods and nonrenewal or termination notice periods; statewide rent caps and just-cause termination requirements (for example Cal. Civ. Code §1947.12 and §1946.2), with local overlays where a city adds its own; entry-notice period; required lease disclosures; and record-retention periods. A property whose state or overlay pack is not active, or whose pack lacks a field an action needs, is held from that deposit, late-fee, notice or lease action with a visible setup gap; no default value is ever substituted. Rule changes are new dated versions, and past decisions keep the version they used. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the same move-out with a $1,800 deposit in a California, a Texas and a Florida property: California returns an itemized statement due in 21 days by personal delivery or first-class mail (Cal. Civ. Code §1950.5(g)); Texas a refund due in 30 days after surrender (Tex. Prop. Code §92.103); Florida a certified-mail notice of claim due in 30 days, or a refund in 15 days with no claim (Fla. Stat. §83.49(3)). Compute a late fee in the Texas property only after rent stays unpaid two full days and within the 12% presumption for a structure of four or fewer units (Tex. Prop. Code §92.019). In a California property, propose a renewal increase above the §1947.12 cap and show it held with the cap calculation, and show a nonrenewal of a resident in occupancy for 12 months held until a §1946.2 just cause is recorded. List all 51 packs with version and review state, remove one field from a pack and show the dependent action blocked, then publish a new pack version and show an earlier disposition keeps its original version.

- **ROLE-001 — Grant staff rights by responsibility.** Owner controls access, integrations, money authorities and cutover. Portfolio managers lease and approve property work within assigned properties and limits; leasing staff manage prospects without bank data; bookkeepers post and reconcile without managing access; accountants read/export and propose close corrections; maintenance coordinators schedule work without approving payouts. Enforce these rights on pages, APIs, exports and search. Exact semantics: each non-owner operator holds one Terrace role (portfolio manager, leasing, bookkeeper, accountant, maintenance coordinator or member) over Base’s sign-in tier (portfolio managers sign in as admin, the others as member) and covers either every portfolio or only the portfolios the owner assigns. The owner alone controls access (invitations, deactivation, roles and portfolio assignments, on Terrace’s and Base’s operator doors alike), integrations, money authorities (the PORT-001 currency confirmation; payouts when they ship) and cutover (move-in import, backup, restore and erasure). One capability table in D1 lists each role’s actions; every command, page tab, API read, download and export checks it for the signed-in operator, and every write rechecks it with the live operator and the role version inside the same D1 batch, so a role or assignment change applies from the next request (a change of sign-in tier also ends that person’s sessions). A record outside the operator’s portfolios answers 404 and never reveals that it exists; an action outside their role answers 403. A tenancy without a unit is reached only by the owner and all-portfolio staff, and installation-wide changes (the company, legal entities, operating accounts, new portfolios and tenancies without a unit) need all-portfolio scope. Portfolio managers manage tenancies, households, renewals and their approval, rent, charge terms and runs, statements, the rent roll and collections, properties and units, inspections, vendors and schedules, and approve maintenance within the owner’s per-property job limit and yearly budget (WORK-003), never access. Leasing staff manage tenancies, households, move-out, transfers, turnovers and renewal proposals, and never see ledgers, receipts, statements, collections, rent-roll money, operating accounts or EINs. Bookkeepers post charges, receipts and corrections, run lease charge terms, prepare arrears follow-ups, and read statements, collections, the rent roll, legal entities and operating accounts, never people or access. Accountants read every ledger and download every statement, rent roll and collections report, and post nothing. Maintenance coordinators run inspections, prepare, quote, reassign and hand off maintenance, record completions, assets, vendors, schedules and turnovers, and never approve spend, set owner limits, post money or change access. Members read their portfolios and prepare renewal, arrears and maintenance proposals. Admins at the upgrade became portfolio managers over every portfolio and members stayed members over every portfolio; an operator invited through Base’s own door gets that same default at first sign-in until the owner sets a role. Every role or assignment change is recorded with who made it and when. Doors not built yet inherit this before they ship: OPS-001 credentials, PORTAL-001 to PORTAL-010 staff screens, LEASE-005 prospects, ACCT-008, ACCT-010 and ACCT-018 payouts, PORT-007 and PORTAL-006 search, OPS-004 integrations, and ROLE-003 per-person approval limits and separate payment authority. **Acceptance evidence:** Exercise each role across two assigned portfolios, directly request another property’s API/download, and attempt a payout as leasing staff and an access change as bookkeeper; every unauthorized path is denied. Until payouts ship (ACCT-008, ACCT-010, ACCT-018 carry the leasing refusal), leasing staff attempt to post money (an external receipt and the charge run) and are refused.

- **ROLE-002 — Isolate residents owners and vendors.** Residents see only their authorized lease/household accounts; owners see only properties and periods they own; vendors see assigned work and their invoices. Invitations, delegated contacts, ownership changes and revocation update rights immediately without exposing other households, owners, screening or bank details. **Acceptance evidence:** Invite a resident, joint owner and vendor, change an assignment and ownership date, then crawl portal pages, direct downloads and APIs before and after revocation.

- **ROLE-003 — Apply dual control and spending limits.** Set owner-approved per-property repair limits, quote requirements, reserves and separate preparation/approval/payment authorities. Agents and preparers cannot approve their own work. Changed amount, payee, bank details or terms invalidate approval; bank-detail changes, including an ACH notification of change (ACCT-018), use independent verification and a separate audit event. SAFE-001 applies before this ships: a bank-detail change needs the changer’s fresh email-link proof. Exact semantics: the owner’s per-job limit and yearly budget (WORK-003) and, beside them, an optional quote requirement (2 to 10 quotes when a job’s estimate is above a set amount) are recorded per property by the owner or a portfolio manager, labelled “recorded by staff for the owner”; the reserve requirement is the management agreement’s (PORT-003). Preparation, approval and payment are separate capabilities in ROLE-001’s table: members, coordinators and agents prepare; portfolio managers and the owner approve; nobody pays yet. Whoever prepared a maintenance job, renewal or arrears proposal never approves it, the owner included, and whoever revises a job’s terms, selects a quote or reassigns its vendor becomes a preparer of those terms; the approval answers 403. The one exception is an installation where no other active operator who did not prepare the terms holds the approval capability: the preparer’s approval is then kept labelled “self-approved: no second person”, rechecked inside the approval’s D1 batch. A job above the quote requirement cannot be approved with fewer quotes recorded. Any change of amount, payee (vendor), scope, title, due date or quote after approval makes a new version that must be approved again (with the owner’s decision again when over the limit or budget) before the packet or handoff (WORK-003). Payments, owner distributions, payee bank details and notifications of change are carried by ACCT-008, ACCT-010 and ACCT-018 (with SAFE-001’s step-up), which apply these rules before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare an over-limit repair and owner distribution, change the payee after approval, and show approval escalation and execution refusal until an independently authorized person approves the exact new inputs. A bank-detail change without a fresh email-link proof is refused (403).

### Leasing acquisition and execution

- **LEASE-001 — Publish property marketing and vacancies.** Publish accessible property/unit pages with accurate availability, rent, fees, deposits, amenities, photos, floorplans and enquiry/application actions. Vacancies come from unit and lease status; managers preview changes and withdraw occupied units. One listing record is the source for every channel: the same unit publishes to the manager’s own site page and, through LEASE-002 and LEASE-003, to Zillow and Apartments.com, and a later edit or withdrawal reaches all three. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish two vacant units, submit an enquiry on mobile, lease one and confirm its vacancy is withdrawn while the other remains available with the approved price and fees. The installation owner records a resident access or erasure request with its external reference (no resident contact by Terrace), downloads that tenancy’s primary-resident access file without other household or owner contacts, and reviews retained classes, dates and reasons. Only the owner places or releases a class or all-record legal hold, with a reason. Erasure requires that request’s current review fingerprint and exact resident email; changed holds or terms require a new review. A lease period or lease/all hold prevents deletion of lease evidence. Once eligible, its originals, photos, work and import copies/search provenance are removed atomically with the restore fence; accounting identity stays while its period or hold applies. After both expire or are released, the tenancy’s personal fields and financial free-text references, fee labels, close references and resident-linked audit source text become an opaque alias/record id; amounts, currencies, dates, financial links, close rows and attributable audit events are kept, and an ordinary update still cannot alter them. The erased tenancy disappears from operational reads; retained accounting remains in exports. The hashed resident-email suppression prevents reimport, and an older archive is refused when the later erasure journal is supplied. No screening records, delivered resident communications, external provider artifacts or external search index exist today: LEASE-009, PORT-007, PORTAL-005, PORTAL-006 and OPS-004 inherit class retention, scoped access/erasure, holds, removal from every stored copy/search/media/provider and the same restore fence before adding them. ### Service boundaries and specialist partners

- **BOUND-001 — Use regulated rails without becoming the custodian.** Terrace owns the complete residential ledger and orchestrates collection, trust-account reconciliation, approved vendor/owner payments and deposit refunds through contracted banks and processors. Banks hold funds; providers perform payment-network functions, identity verification and required onboarding. For outbound ACH the manager’s bank remains the originating bank: Terrace prepares and reconciles the NACHA files (ACCT-018) and never holds funds. This boundary does not exclude online collection or trust accounting. **Acceptance evidence:** Onboard a manager’s processor and bank account, collect rent, reconcile trust subledgers and execute an approved payout; show funds remain at the bank/provider and Terrace records authoritative reconciled outcomes.

- **BOUND-002 — Keep housing legal tax and insurance decisions human.** TransUnion supplies reports; a manager decides applicants. Counsel approves jurisdiction-specific lease/notice/deposit rule packs and eviction/service actions; accountants review taxes and filings; MSI/other insurers issue policies. Track all referrals and responses in Terrace without claiming to be a bureau, lawyer, CPA or insurer. **Acceptance evidence:** Hold a disputed screening result, uncertain notice rule and rejected tax filing; refer each to its authorized professional, record the response and resume only the explicitly approved action.

- **BOUND-003 — Coordinate emergency and physical work with people.** Local licensed vendors, the manager’s on-call service, emergency responders and utilities perform field work and emergency response. Terrace delivers urgent instructions/escalations, acknowledges handoff and checks subsequent resolution; it never presents an unattended bot or successful notification as physical emergency assistance. **Acceptance evidence:** Receive a safety request, provide reviewed 911/utility guidance, obtain a human on-call acknowledgment and keep the case open until authorized resolution evidence and resident follow-up are recorded.

- **BOUND-004 — Stay residential: no commercial leases, CAM or HOA dues.** Terrace manages residential rentals only. It has no commercial lease type, no CAM reconciliation and no homeowners’-association dues, assessments or association accounting, and says so in setup (the app’s first-run portfolio setup, which is the empty Tenancies page after Finish setup, and the Move in screen), the documentation and its public pages, so a buyer with commercial or association work learns the limit before importing. Commercial CAM recoveries and project-based affordable housing remain with specialist systems through OPS-008, and a residential owner’s own HOA dues remain an ordinary recurring vendor bill (ACCT-007), not association accounting; ACCT-007 carries that acceptance, because vendor bills ship with it. An import that carries commercial lease records holds them as exceptions rather than loading them as residential tenancies. In Terrace tenancy CSV v1 the optional `lease_type` column marks a row: empty or `residential` (any letter case) imports, and any other value is held as a rejected exception naming that value, so the reviewed file cannot be approved and nothing from it loads until the row is removed. Each incumbent importer (MOVE-002 through MOVE-007) inherits this hold for the lease or property type its source carries before it ships (MOVE-001). **Acceptance evidence:** Search the app, API and setup for any commercial-lease, CAM or association-dues feature and find none; read the stated limit in setup and the docs; import a source file containing a commercial lease and show it held as an exception.

## Push further — accountable portfolio agents

These agents serve the same residential job on real operating records. Authority, human decisions, completion evidence and safe stopping conditions are part of each journey.

- **AGENT-001 — Run repair intake through owner-statement resolution.** A portfolio agent joins resident intake, asset history, access constraints, vendor qualifications, quotes, approved spend, appointment, completion evidence, resident reply, invoice and owner ledger. It books within human-approved authority, chases blocked work and confirms repair with the resident, then matches the bill and reconciles its statement effect. People approve vendors, spend changes and payments; emergencies escalate immediately. **Acceptance evidence:** Give the agent a leaking tap, approved vendor/spend envelope and resident availability; interrupt booking, change the invoice and receive a failed completion check. It resumes safely, reopens the repair, holds the mismatch and closes only after confirmed repair and reconciled owner cost.

- **AGENT-002 — Plan vacancy recovery with cash trade-offs.** An agent combines lease ends, condition evidence, make-ready dependencies, vendor quotes, lead funnel and explicitly dated market assumptions to compare repair, listing and offer plans by vacancy days and cash impact. It follows the approved plan to signed lease and move-in, revising forecasts as facts change. Managers approve capital work, pricing, offers, legal terms and applicant decisions. **Acceptance evidence:** Present two turnover plans with source-linked cost/vacancy assumptions, approve one, delay a vendor and recalculate; follow the revised authorized plan to a human-approved applicant and executed lease, comparing actual vacancy/cash with forecast.

- **AGENT-003 — Investigate deposit and close exceptions.** An agent joins lease deposit obligations, processor settlements, bank feeds and beneficiary subledgers to find missing deposits, duplicates, owner misallocations and stale outstanding items. It assembles source-linked correction proposals and pursues missing evidence before close. Bookkeepers approve corrections; deposit deductions, transfers and payouts retain separate human approvals. **Acceptance evidence:** Introduce a duplicate receipt, missing deposit and repair charged to the wrong owner; have the agent explain each difference from source evidence, propose corrections and reconcile only approved entries while unresolved differences block close.

- **AGENT-004 — Pursue arrears to a permitted resolution.** An agent tracks consented attempts, replies, promised payments, plans and actual settled receipts, proposes the next permitted contact and follows through until paid, a plan is met or an accountable professional accepts handoff. It pauses on dispute, accommodation, contact limits or changed balance. Managers approve concessions/notices/money movement; counsel controls legal escalation. **Acceptance evidence:** Work late rent with a promise that fails, a later returned payment and a disputed fee; show permitted next actions and holds, obtain human approvals, then verify settled resolution rather than treating a promise or submitted debit as payment.

- **AGENT-005 — Steward renewals through executed future rent.** An agent reads lease dates, current terms, communication history, condition and approved vacancy/cash assumptions, prepares sourced renewal alternatives and follows approved offers, replies, signatures and future charge setup. It surfaces retention/concession trade-offs and pauses if terms or authority change; people approve pricing, legal terms and notices. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare renewal options, approve one, receive a counteroffer and require new approval; collect all signatures, verify future charges match the executed terms and escalate an unanswered offer before the deadline.

- **AGENT-006 — Compare inspection evidence without inventing damage.** An agent compares dated original entry/exit/repair photos, video, checklist notes and asset history to highlight possible changes with exact source locations and uncertainty. It proposes inspection follow-up, repair or deposit-review items; a qualified person decides condition, wear, liability and deductions. Insufficient or contradictory evidence remains visible. **Acceptance evidence:** Compare an unchanged fixture, documented damage and ambiguous low-light image; inspect source-linked findings, reject an unsupported deduction and carry a human-approved repair into work with original media retained.

- **AGENT-007 — Learn operating risks from verified outcomes.** An agent analyzes vacancy, repeat repairs, resident-confirmed outcomes, collections, cash, reconciliation exceptions and vendor reliability to suggest preventive tasks, staffing and workflow changes. It separates hypotheses from measured outcomes and forecasts cost/benefit. Managers review the proposal and authority before any changed vendor, budget, fee, contact or workflow is activated. **Acceptance evidence:** Detect repeated HVAC callbacks and a leasing bottleneck, show source cases and quantified assumptions, approve one preventive plan and compare later actual outcomes without silently modifying the other workflow.

- **AGENT-008 — Keep agent authority and evidence inspectable.** Every agent workload exposes purpose, permitted data/actions, human approvals, next step, due time, attempts, replies, costs and outcome evidence. People can pause, cancel or reassign; revocation stops queued execution. Untrusted resident/vendor documents cannot expand authority or instruct payments, and the agent cannot approve its own proposal. **Acceptance evidence:** Embed a payment instruction in an invoice, revoke the agent during a queued booking and cancel a follow-up. Show ignored document instructions, stopped execution and a complete human-readable action history with no self-approval.

- **AGENT-009 — Weigh each arrears case in cash and vacant days.** For each delinquent household an agent compares a payment plan with a pay-or-quit notice (and waiting) by expected cash and vacant days: rent at risk, plan receipts by date weighted by the household’s payment history, the notice period and eventual turnover timeline from the property’s PORT-008 pack, expected vacant days at the unit’s rent, make-ready and leasing cost, and the resulting expected loss for each option. It shows its dated assumptions, sources and confidence, drafts the preferred plan or notice, and stops for a person to approve and send it (PAY-011, PORTAL-009). It never sends, serves or posts ledger entries itself, pauses on dispute, accommodation or contact limits (AGENT-004) and later compares the chosen option’s actual cash and vacancy with its forecast. **Acceptance evidence:** Take one household with a plausible plan and one that has missed a plan; show each case’s plan-versus-notice comparison in cash and vacant days with its assumptions, change the vacancy assumption and watch the recommendation recalculate, approve and send the plan for one and the notice for the other as a person, and show nothing was sent before approval and the forecast compared with the actual outcome afterwards.
